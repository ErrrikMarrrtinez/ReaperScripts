# Advanced Align · experimental, 0.13.4

Classic offers opt-in [Hybrid timing](HYBRID.md) in 0.13.5: fragment movement, quiet trimming and attack-preserving compression.

## 0.13.4: grouped items in Advanced, crossfade overlaps, looped tails, optimizer

- **Grouped roles in Advanced.** The Advanced reader, ownership checks, planner, validator, marker reduction and renderer accept a reference or dub made of several items on one track. Item edges and each item's own source knots are mandatory knots; each item's fades stay rigid against that item's edges; source time is checked per item; the renderer writes, reads back and rolls back every item separately.
- **Crossfade overlaps.** Consecutive items may overlap by up to half the shorter item and at most 2 s; their audio is summed there (as REAPER plays it), the same map moves both, so the overlap survives. A larger overlap (one take mostly inside another) is still rejected as stacked takes.
- **Looped tails.** An item that plays past the end of its file with Loop source on now offers *Trim looped tails*: the item is shortened to its file end (existing stretch markers kept, end marker placed exactly at the file end) in one Undo step, then the selection is captured again.
- **Guided optimizer.** The guided matcher now feeds the Advanced optimizer the smoothed Classic map (full strength, zero tolerance, at least 40 ms smoothing) instead of the raw 10 ms DTW staircase, whose local slopes of 0.5 and 2 violated the stretch limit everywhere and could exhaust the iteration budget (`solver_not_converged`). Guided plans get a 600-iteration budget; if it is still not certified, the last iterate projected onto the exact constraints and independently validated is used and the lane says *approximate optimizer*. A plan failure with a correspondence now reads *Calculation failed*, not *No accepted timing*.

## 0.13.3: grouped items and the guided matcher

**Grouped roles.** With *Group items per track* on (default), consecutive selected items on one track form one reference or one dub: one virtual timeline from the first item's start to the last item's end, with digital silence in the gaps. Classic reads every item through its own accessor into that timeline, aligns it as a whole, keeps every item edge as a marker knot, then slices the map back per item: each item gets its own position, length and edge-anchored markers; gaps stretch within the same stretch limit and items never overlap after apply. Restore, change detection, group identity and the volume transport work per item. Items must share the channel count; a reference may be grouped as well. Grouping off restores the previous behaviour (every selected item is its own dub).

**Guided matcher (Advanced default).** The strict candidate matcher, measured on a real four-dub session, declared 70–80 % of content ambiguous, accepted 50–300 ms islands and then could not plan most of them, so the applied map stayed within a few milliseconds of identity while Classic moved content by up to 160 ms. Advanced now defaults to *Matcher: Classic path (guided)*: the Classic DTW path on Classic features gives one dense correspondence over the whole dub (unsupported audio interpolates through the path, head and tail continue at the original rate), and the Advanced planner, validators and renderer shape it under the stretch limits, pins, protected intervals, fades and verified writes. Replayed on that session, the guided plan stays within a median of 2 ms and p95 of 12 ms of the Classic map, with 230–270 markers per dub. The strict matcher remains selectable as *strict candidates (experimental)*; its per-dub work caps no longer share one batch budget, and one dub's budget or evidence failure no longer fails the others.

**Fades.** The planner now knows the item fades: fade intervals keep rate 1 relative to their item edge, their boundaries are mandatory knots, and when quiet background cannot absorb a correction before a fade, the item edge moves rigidly instead of the renderer refusing the batch ("The timing map deforms the item fade-in"). Export Align case no longer fails on plans (sparse knot flags are serialized as objects) and records fades and the last error.

Align now has two selectable algorithms. **Classic** retains the 0.12.1 feature extraction, DTW, smoothing and timing-map code. **Advanced** is a separate implementation of the supplied phrase-alignment specification. It has numerical and simulated-host validation; it has not yet been validated by listening to representative vocal sessions in REAPER. Experimental does not mean better in every case.

## Try it

Close and rerun `mrtnz_Vocaligner.lua`. In Align, capture the reference and dubs, select **Advanced (experimental)**, then press **Analyze**. Accepted timing is applied automatically. Ambiguous or unsupported events remain at captured timing; their status is shown on the lanes. Classic's existing low-match auto-apply behavior remains unchanged.

Each algorithm remembers its own settings and analysis for the captured group. Switching algorithms changes the displayed preview only. If the other algorithm has no cached features and the take is already aligned, use **Restore**, then **Analyze**. This prevents an algorithm from analyzing its own processed timing. Planned, Captured and Applied choose the displayed timing; they do not switch the audible project state.

Advanced applies timing controls when released. Strength 0 restores captured timing exactly. Strength blends displacement; Tolerance removes small corrections before blending. Marker density controls map reduction within independently checked error limits. Smoothing regularizes displacement, and stretch limits constrain adjacent intervals. Search/profile changes rematch cached features. Optional pitch evidence reads F0/voicing on a 40 ms grid, reusing acoustic features when available; it may require Restore and Analyze if timing has already changed. This is separate from the Pitch editor and does not correct pitch. Its F0 distance has 5% nominal weight for Vocal doubles and zero weight for Harmony/Speech; voicing still informs event classification. Reports include extraction time and reliable pitch-frame counts.

The manual-constraints panel offers hard/soft synchronization points, protected intervals and excluded matching intervals. Enter dub times relative to its captured start and Guide times in absolute project seconds. Pins are anchored to source content and follow Strength while bypassing Tolerance. Protect keeps captured timing; Exclude removes matching evidence. Incompatible constraints fail visibly. Editing is numeric in this prototype; dragging pins on the waveform is not implemented.

**Report… → Export Align case** on the Align tab writes JSON, feature CSVs and a completion manifest under REAPER's `Data/VocalignerAlignCases`. By default it contains no audio, media paths or take names. It includes settings, source knots, constraints, correspondence evidence, plans and available native readback. The file-size/FNV checks detect accidental corruption; they are not cryptographic authentication. There is no in-product case importer yet.

Since 0.13.1, Cancel keeps completed feature reads for this export. Reports show the live/last calculation phase, active dub, candidate, counters, CPU work and elapsed time even when no result has been published. Nested candidate passes now advance only their share of the overall bar; they cannot promote the first dub to 98%.

The lane's percentage means **accepted content**, not acoustic similarity or a probability. `Ambiguous - no timing change` is a finished calculation. `Partial match` means only the accepted regions were planned. During computation, the lane identifies EEL or Lua. Copy report and Export Align case include feature/Pitch statistics and local consensus evidence.

item-chunk roundtrips may recreate PCM handles and round marker coordinates;
the bridge now compares stable source identity and numeric geometry. It checks
every part of grouped dubs, transports each part's gain and recipes on reapply

This fix was reproduced and checked in the host model; native REAPER validation

De-ess, Breaths, Compressor and Gate keep their current tool order, presets, per-item settings and single composed Volume envelope. A verified cleanup edit may advance the Volume owner without replacing Align's immutable timing capture. Both Align engines transport the current owned gain through source coordinates when changing timing. Restore returns timing while retaining that gain. Cleanup recipes are transported with it; analysis and detector caches are invalidated when the audio timing changes, and are read again when needed. Undo/Redo can recover the corresponding in-session cleanup state.

This interoperability applies to known outputs from these tools. Unrecognized external changes still require a new capture. Guide edits are never silently accepted. The renderer checks ownership before writing, verifies native results and rolls back failures. Advanced keeps full item-state snapshots for rollback; a host failure that also prevents rollback is reported as requiring REAPER Undo.

Advanced currently supports a single active take with a forward, nonlooped, piecewise-linear source map. It rejects curved markers, reverse/section sources, incompatible source endpoints, inactive additional takes, owned Pitch automation and other nonempty take/FX envelopes. Take Volume is explicitly transported. Track automation remains in project time. A deformation through an item fade is rejected; a rigid move can retain a fade. The fixed 32 kHz analysis adapter rejects accessor play rates that cannot provide an integral read rate, rather than silently changing its time grid.

## Implementation and bounded work

Product code uses Lua, REAPER accessors, native FFT and an embedded ReaImGui EEL kernel. It does not start Python or another audio process. Features, matching, timing plans and application have separate cache/version boundaries. EEL evaluates acoustic distances and dynamic-programming transitions in blocks of 256 cells. Packed trace data stays native; Lua reads only the winning path and retains constraints, confidence, planning, cancellation and all project writes. Native storage is capped at 64 MiB. A compilation failure selects the retained Lua implementation and is visible in diagnostics.

The matcher uses activity/phrase segmentation, bounded coarse candidates, positive query-normalized gap costs, packed finite-state paths, phrase ordering and ambiguity checks. Unmatched content remains fixed. Confirmed quiet regions can provide bounded transition shoulders. Since algorithm version 2, an ambiguous whole phrase can retain contiguous regions of at least 150 ms where every eligible, complete near-optimal candidate agrees within 20 ms. Missing support or a conflicting local replay removes that region. These regions are labelled `local_consensus`; this does not lower the whole-phrase confidence gates. If an accepted region borders unknown vocal audio, the planner can ease to a fixed endpoint inside the accepted region. It does not stretch neighboring disputed audio to create a transition.

The planner solves a constrained quadratic problem in source coordinates, preserves original source knots, validates rate bounds and constraints independently, then reduces markers subject to map-error limits. Exact baseline/no-op cases bypass fitting. Feature memory, duration, state count, trace size, solver iterations and native writes have explicit caps. A budget failure returns an error and does not substitute a lower-quality result silently.

The solver reuses exact gap minima, precomputes legal gap lengths and uniform-grid transition costs, and shares an acoustic pair cache across candidate passes. The cache has a separate conservative 64 MiB estimate cap and is released after matching. `states` counts states actually evaluated; impossible gap lengths are not charged as if they ran. Packed traceback still reserves the original 43/47-byte cell format. A frozen-solver comparison verifies exact scores, paths and tie-breaking in regular and macro-gap cases. The existing fractional-sample source tail accepted by REAPER capture is preserved, with no extension beyond the captured endpoint.

Two departures from the document's final numerical defaults are deliberate and versioned:

- Guide-only material between separate query phrases is not penalized as missing query content when the intervening dub interval is confirmed background. Penalizing it made a two-phrase sparse backing select only one perfect match. Internal gaps, query skips, ordering and tempo checks remain.
- The QP uses exact fixed-difference condensation, an LDL warm start, 1.6 over-relaxation and bounded residual-based penalty adjustment. It separates independent components only where both the objective and constraints provably decouple. Once ADMM identifies active bounds, an exact equality solve may finish early only after checking the original inequalities, stationarity and multiplier signs. Each component retains the 2,000-iteration cap, and all share the total work cap. The objective and strict residual tolerances are unchanged.

## Validation and remaining work

The external research harness checks feature/FFT behavior, gain and stereo robustness, synthetic identity/delay/repeated-attack/sparse-phrase/long-word cases, hard constraints, protected intervals and source-map reduction. A brute-force oracle covers 96 small dynamic-programming cases (16,330 enumerated paths). Independent SciPy SLSQP comparisons cover 30 general and 60 active-bound quadratic problems, including 54 cases completed by the certified equality refinement. These verify implementation properties, not perceptual quality.

The actual standalone WDL EEL JIT passes 24 frozen-solver cases and 120 additional exact Lua comparisons covering real distance terms, pitch/voicing flags, fractional frame masses, missing/silent/unreliable data, hard pins, exclusions and forbidden alternatives. Native memory/work guards and cancellation during EEL calculation are checked. Cache/full Pitch extraction agrees exactly on a fixed PCM fixture, and turning Pitch evidence off restores the acoustic-only features.

Simulated native APIs exercise apply/readback, immutable captures, zero/Restore, A/B switching, cache reuse, stale requests, failure isolation, marker failure, rollback failure and shared cleanup transport. The Advanced controller is also exercised with the actual EEL kernel. Existing Pitch/cleanup regressions and the standalone WDL EEL compressor/gate kernel are checked against the combined tree. Native REAPER/ReaImGui interaction, real-world timing accuracy, stretch artifacts and listening quality remain unverified.

The next quality gate is a held-out vocal corpus with manual reference landmarks, repeated attacks, sparse backing phrases and already-aligned takes, followed by native listening comparisons. Pitch-envelope retiming and a move/resize renderer remain separate future work. They are not available engine choices.
